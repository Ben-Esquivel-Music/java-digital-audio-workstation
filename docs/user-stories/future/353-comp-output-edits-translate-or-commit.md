# Comp Output Edits: Translate Into the Comp or Commit It

## Motivation

Story 328 made comping real. A track's `TakeComping` holds the take lanes and the `CompRegion` selection, and `CompileCompAction` compiles that selection onto the main lane as file-referencing slices. Swipes (`SetCompRegionAction` through `CompToolHandler`) and lane auditions (Alt-click solo, and a main-lane click that ends a solo) recompile at once. Review finding F4 on PR #984 found that comp ownership went stale after a Scissors split. The fix gives every clip a compile puts on the main lane a comp-owner tag on `AudioClip`, with that compile's pass of the track's `TakeComping` as the owner:

- `splitAt` halves and trimmed remnant pieces (`AudioClip.trimmedCopy`) keep the tag.
- `duplicate()` copies (Duplicate, Paste) drop it.
- In-place edits keep the object and its tag.
- `CompileCompAction` works out what the comp occupies from the live track. It removes every clip tagged as this comp's output, of any pass, then adds the fresh compile, whose slices `TakeComping.compile()` tags. The remembered `TakeComping.getCompClips()` list is gone.
- Output of an earlier pass never re-enters its track: `Track.addClip` refuses a clip `TakeComping.isSupersededOutput` holds for, so an undo that puts back a clip snapshot (an erase, a cut, a paste-over, a crop, a move to another track) after a swipe, a swipe's undo or an audition cannot leave the comp playing twice.
- A comp clip moved to another track keeps its tag, which is inert there: only its own comping's track scans for it (`compOutputOn`) or refuses it (`Track.addClip`), and `TakeComping.isCompOutput` holds only for that comping's passes. On the other track it therefore behaves as a user clip: a swipe there never touches it. Moved back with no recompile in between, it is comp output again and the next swipe replaces it. The move clears the tag only when the destination track's comping holds the clip as superseded output (`isSupersededOutput`), so a deliberate move of stale output lands as a user clip; the move's undo puts the saved tag back before it re-adds the clip through `Track.addClip`. Whether a move clears the tag is decided once, on its first execute, and a redo replays that decision.
- The pass advances only in `CompileCompAction` (`TakeComping.compile()` is package-private), so every comp clip on its own comping's track is output of the current pass, or derived from it by an edit that keeps the tag. That is why a split's second half, a glue of two halves and a fresh clip are never refused.

The invariant that fix documents is: **the main lane is always exactly what the comping state says it is.**

**Already landed.** Acceptance criterion 1 (a split is audio-neutral through a swipe) and the superseded-output fence above landed in the story-328 review rounds of PR #984. This story builds on both and keeps them as regression criteria.

That invariant has a cost, accepted for story 328 and closed by this story. The comp model cannot see most manual edits of comp output: moving it in time, slipping it, trimming or cropping it, deleting or erasing it, pasting over it, gluing it to a user clip, or changing its gain or fades. The next swipe or lane audition replaces such an edit without warning, and an audition does so with no undo entry:

- **Move or slip:** the clip keeps its tag, so the recompile removes it and puts the slice back where the region says it belongs.
- **Cross-track move:** the moved clip behaves as a user clip on the other track (its tag is inert there), and the recompile brings the range back on the source track, so the move acts as a copy.
- **Cross-track move, then a drag home after a recompile:** the clip's tag is superseded output on its home track by then, so the move clears it and the clip lands as a user clip over the slice the recompile put back. The range plays twice (coherently, about +6 dB, when it is the same lane at the same position) until the clip is deleted, which breaks criterion 8.
- **Delete or erase:** the region still selects that range, so the recompile brings the audio back.
- **Edge trim:** the recompile restores the full region.
- **Paste Over and Crop** (the track menu's `ClipEditOperations.pasteOver` and `ClipEditOperations.crop`, daw-app): the pieces of comp output they leave are `AudioClip.trimmedCopy` remnants that keep the tag. A crop behaves like an edge trim, so the next swipe restores the full region. A Paste Over's pasted clip is a user clip, so the next swipe replaces the remnants with a whole slice that plays under the pasted clip.
- **Glue:** `GlueClipsAction` only joins clips of the same source. A glue of two clips of this comp's output (the halves of a split) keeps the tag, and the next swipe replaces it with no change to the audio: it is neutral, like a split. A glue of comp output with a user clip of the same take (a duplicate) is untagged: it survives the recompile, and the slice the region compiles under it plays that range twice. That case falls under this story's commit/refuse rule.
- **Gain and fade envelopes:** these live on the clip object, so they are lost when the compile replaces the clip.

Render-in-place shows the same gap from the other direction. When `RenderInPlaceAction` replaces a comped track's clips on the source track, the rendered clip is untagged and comping is still active. The next swipe lays comp output over the render, and the track plays the take twice.

Every one of these cases can be undone, and none of them is what the engineer asked for. Two common DAW behaviours set the expectation. In a take-folder DAW, editing the comp result either edits the comp (deselecting a range leaves a gap, and dragging a comp boundary moves it) or is refused while the comp is live. In a playlist-lane DAW, the comp is copied to the main playlist as ordinary audio, and later edits never get overwritten behind the engineer's back.

## Goals

Translate an edit of comp output into the comp model where the model can express it. Otherwise, commit the comp in the same undoable step that performs the edit.

| Edit on comp output | Behaviour |
|---------------------|-----------|
| **Split** (Scissors, `SplitClipAction`; Split at Playhead) and a **glue of two clips of the same comp** (`GlueClipsAction`) | Audio-neutral. Both halves, or the glued clip, stay comp output, which is already true after the F4 fix. The next swipe recompiles the range unchanged. |
| **Delete / Eraser** (`RemoveClipAction`, the Eraser tool's `handleEraserPress`) | Becomes a gap in the comp. Every `CompRegion` over the removed clip's range is removed or clipped to exclude that range. The next swipe keeps the gap, like "deselect" in a take-folder DAW. The engineer fills the gap by swiping a lane over it again. |
| **Edge trim** (`ClipEdgeTrimAction`, `TrimClipAction`) | Moves the matching `CompRegion` bound, like dragging a comp boundary in a take-folder DAW. Trimming the start of a slice moves its region's start, and trimming the end moves its region's end. A trim that shortens a slice leaves a gap beside it. A trim that extends a slice moves the boundary it shares with the adjacent region, clipping that region, and is clamped to the lane's recorded audio. |
| **Crop** (`ClipEditOperations.crop`, daw-app) | Translates as moving both region bounds: every `CompRegion` is clipped to the crop selection, and a region wholly outside it is removed. |
| **Slice fades and clip gain** (`FadeClipAction`; `NormalizeClipGainAction` and a flat gain change) | Translates into the comp: a `CompRegion` carries fade-in and fade-out lengths and a gain offset, which `compile()` applies to the slice it compiles. A fade at a comp boundary or a level match between slices therefore survives every later swipe, and comping stays live. **Fades at a shared comp boundary** (the fade-out of one slice and the fade-in of the next where two regions meet) are one crossfade of length L centred on the boundary. L is the longer of the two fade lengths when the engineer set both and they differ, and the one length set when only one side has a fade. `compile()` extends each of the two slices past the boundary by L/2 as a handle, so the slices overlap under the crossfade instead of butting with both gains at 0. A handle is clamped to the audio its lane recorded; when one clamps, both handles shrink to the shorter available handle, so the overlap stays centred on the boundary and both curves span the same window. With no handle on one side, the boundary falls back to a short butt-splice fade: a fade-out and a fade-in that meet at the boundary without overlapping. The law is equal-power by default between different lanes, whose takes are uncorrelated: across the overlap, for t from 0 to 1, the outgoing slice's gain is cos(πt/2) and the incoming slice's sin(πt/2). **A gain offset follows its lane, by intersection**: it was set for the take that plays there. After a swipe, every part of the timeline that still plays the same lane keeps that lane's offset (a remainder the swipe clipped and a same-lane extension of the region inherit it), and only the range where the lane changes resets, playing the new lane at 0 dB offset. A normalize is stored as the static dB offset it produced and is not re-evaluated. |
| **Move in time** (`MoveClipAction`, `GroupMoveClipsAction`), **slip** (`SlipClipAction`), **glue of comp output with a user clip** (`GlueClipsAction`), **a shaped gain envelope** (`SetClipGainEnvelopeAction` with breakpoints that are not a flat offset), **cross-track move** (`CrossTrackMoveAction`), **render-in-place onto the source track** (`RenderInPlaceAction`) | Cannot be expressed in the comp model, so it **commits the comp**. In the same undoable step as the edit, every clip of this comp's output becomes an ordinary clip (owner tag cleared), and the comp is marked *committed*. After that, a swipe or lane audition on the track is **refused** with a visible status message: *"comp committed by an edit — undo to resume comping"*. The swipe never layers new slices over committed audio. |
| **Copy / paste / duplicate** (`DuplicateClipsAction`, `PasteClipsAction`, and the track menu's Paste Over, `ClipEditOperations.pasteOver`) | The copy is an ordinary clip, which is already true because `duplicate()` drops the tag. If a later recompile would overlap a user clip (an untagged clip on the main lane) inside the comp's range, the same rule applies: a swipe is refused with the committed-comp message, or the edit that created the overlap commits the comp. The comp never silently doubles a user clip. |

Further goals:

- **Committed is a state of the comp, not a deletion.** A committed comp keeps its lanes and its `CompRegion` selection, so undoing the committing edit returns the track to live comping with that selection intact. Uncommitting by any route other than undo is out of scope (see Non-Goals).
- **Undo and redo restore everything together.** Each translated or committing edit is one undo entry, a `CompoundUndoableAction` where the edit and the comp change are composed. Undoing or redoing it restores the clips, their owner tags, the comp regions and the committed flag as one unit. Undo never restores the main lane from a stale clip snapshot. As in story 328, the comp output is recompiled from the live comping state, so an audition between an edit and its undo leaves nothing orphaned or doubled.
- **Auditions stay outside undo.** Lane solos and main-lane clicks keep recompiling directly through `CompileCompAction.execute()`. They are never undo entries. On a committed comp they are refused like swipes, and on a live comp they never commit anything.
- **Render-in-place covered by the commit rule.** Rendering a comped track in place onto its own track commits the comp in the render's undo entry, so the next swipe is refused instead of doubling the render. Rendering to a new track leaves the source comp live, because the render is an ordinary clip on another track.
- **Refusals are visible and specific.** A refused swipe or audition posts the committed-comp message through the app's existing status-message surface, and it changes no clip, region, tag or undo history.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. Splitting comp output, then swiping, leaves the same audio at the same timeline positions as swiping without the split. *(Already met by the story-328 review rounds — `TakeCompingCompileTest.aSwipeAfterAScissorsSplitOfTheCompReplacesBothHalves`; kept as a regression criterion.)*
2. Deleting or erasing a comp-output clip removes or clips the `CompRegion`s over its range in the same undo entry. The next swipe on another range leaves that range silent.
3. Edge-trimming a comp-output clip moves exactly the matching `CompRegion` bound in the same undo entry. The next swipe on another range keeps the trimmed bound.
4. Moving, slipping, gluing to a user clip, giving a shaped gain envelope to, or cross-track moving a comp-output clip, or rendering the comped track in place onto itself, commits the comp in the same undo entry. A slice fade or a clip-gain change does not commit: it is stored on the matching `CompRegion` and the next swipe keeps it, except that the range where a swipe changes the lane that plays resets to 0 dB offset, while every part still playing the same lane keeps it. Fades at a shared comp boundary compile as one crossfade centred on the boundary, whose two gains are complementary at every sample of the overlap under the equal-power law, except the butt-splice fallback when one side has no handle (see Goals). Afterwards no main-lane clip carries this comp's owner tag, and the comp reports itself committed.
5. On a committed comp, a swipe, a lane solo and a main-lane click are refused. Each posts *"comp committed by an edit — undo to resume comping"* and changes no clip, tag, region or undo entry.
6. Undoing a committing edit restores the edit's clips with their owner tags, restores the comp's regions, and clears the committed flag. The next swipe recompiles normally. Redo re-commits.
7. Pasting or duplicating a clip into the comp's range never results in a recompile that plays the pasted clip and a comp slice over the same range. The overlap is resolved by the commit/refuse rule.
8. **Invariant.** After any sequence of edits, swipes, auditions, undos and redos, two things hold. No main-lane time range plays two sources from the same take stack. No user edit is overwritten unless it was committed or the overwriting action was refused.
9. Every case in criteria 1-7 keeps its result when an audition happens between the edit and its undo, and between the undo and its redo.

## Goals — Tests

These tests are binding acceptance criteria. They run headless against `daw-core` comping and undo types plus the `daw-app` interaction seams (`ClipInteractionController`, `CompToolHandler`), and none of them assumes a non-Windows environment. Each scenario runs edit → audition → undo → audition → redo and asserts the main lane, tags, regions and committed flag at every step.

- **Split then swipe** (its split-neutrality is already met, criterion 1; the lane-0 remainders it expects depend on the partial-swipe open question in Technical Notes): a two-lane comp, with lane 0 selected over bars 1-4. Split the comp output at bar 2, then swipe lane 1 over bar 3. The main lane holds lane 0 for bars 1-2 and bar 4 and lane 1 for bar 3, with every piece tagged. Each step is undone and redone to the same state.
- **Erase then swipe keeps the gap**: erase the comp-output clip that covers bar 2, then swipe lane 1 over bar 4. Bar 2 is silent on the main lane, and the regions no longer cover bar 2. Undoing the erase restores the region and recompiles, rather than re-adding the erased clip. Redo removes the region and recompiles again.
- **Delete keeps the gap through an audition**: delete a comp-output clip, solo lane 1 (the whole lane plays), then click the main lane. The comp returns with the gap intact, and the audition added no undo entry.
- **Edge trim moves the region bound**: trim the end of a slice from bar 3 back to bar 2.5. The matching `CompRegion` ends at bar 2.5, no other region changes, and a later swipe elsewhere keeps 2.5. Undo restores the bound to bar 3.
- **Move commits, and the next swipe is refused**: move a comp-output clip one bar later. The clip sits at its new position untagged, the comp is committed, and a swipe on lane 1 is refused with the committed-comp status message. The main lane, the regions and the undo stack are unchanged by the refused swipe.
- **Undo of the move resumes comping**: undo the move from the previous scenario. The clip is back at its original position with its tag, the comp is no longer committed, and a swipe on lane 1 now recompiles. Redo re-commits, and the swipe is refused again.
- **Every non-expressible edit commits**: slip, a glue of comp output with a user clip, a shaped gain envelope and a cross-track move each commit the comp in one undo entry, and each one's undo resumes comping. The scenarios are parameterized over the actions listed in Goals.
- **Fades and gain translate**: a fade-in on a slice, a fade-out on another, and a normalize or flat gain change on a third are stored on their `CompRegion`s. A swipe elsewhere recompiles every one of those slices with its fades and gain, and the comp is not committed. Undo restores each region's attributes and recompiles.
- **A boundary fade is an equal-power crossfade**: two lanes carrying distinct constant signals meet at a comp boundary, and the engineer fades out the first slice and fades in the second there, with two different lengths. After a swipe elsewhere, the two slices overlap by L, the longer length, centred on the boundary, and at every sample of the overlap the two gains are complementary under the equal-power law: g_out² + g_in² = 1 within 1e-6. A fade set on one side only gives the same overlap at that length.
- **A clamped boundary fade stays centred**: as above, but the first lane's recorded audio ends L/4 past the boundary. Both handles shrink to L/4, the overlap is L/2 centred on the boundary, and the gains are complementary at every sample of it within the same tolerance. With no recorded audio past the boundary on one side, the boundary is a butt splice: no overlap, a short fade-out ending at the boundary and a short fade-in starting there.
- **Gain follows its lane, by intersection**: a gain offset set on a slice of lane 0 survives a swipe elsewhere. A swipe that puts lane 1 over the whole region resets it there, so lane 1 plays at 0 dB offset; undoing the swipe restores lane 0 and its offset. **Partial overlap:** a swipe of lane 1 over the middle of that lane-0 region plays lane 1 at 0 dB offset there, and the lane-0 remainders on both sides keep the offset. **Same-lane extension:** a swipe of lane 0 that extends the region past its end plays the extension at the region's offset as well. A normalize is stored as a static dB offset: changing the take's audio afterwards does not change it.
- **Crop moves both bounds**: crop the comp to bars 2-3. The regions are clipped to bars 2-3, and a later swipe elsewhere keeps the crop. Undo restores the regions.
- **Edge trim into the neighbour**: extend a slice's end past its region into the adjacent region. The shared boundary moves and clips the adjacent region; an extension past the lane's recorded audio is clamped to it.
- **Swipe-interleaved undo, for every edit**: for every edit in the Goals table, run edit → swipe → undo swipe → undo edit → redo → redo, and assert at each step that no main-lane span plays two sources from the same take stack and that the regions and committed flag match the step.
- **Paste over the comp range**: copy an unrelated clip and paste it inside the comp's range. The pasted clip is untagged. The next swipe that would compile a slice over it is refused with the committed-comp message, or, when the paste committed the comp, the swipe is refused because the comp is committed. Either way, no range plays both the pasted clip and a slice. Undo of the paste returns the comp to live and the swipe recompiles.
- **Render-in-place then swipe**: render a comped track in place onto its own track. The rendered clip replaces the comp output, and the comp is committed in the render's undo entry. A swipe is refused, so the take never plays twice. Undoing the render restores the tagged comp output and live comping. Rendering to a new track leaves the source comp live, and its swipes still recompile.
- **Invariant fuzz**: a seeded random sequence of edits, swipes, solos, main-lane clicks, undos and redos over a three-lane comp. After every step, no main-lane beat is covered by two clips from the same take stack, and no clip the sequence edited was replaced unless the comp was committed or the step was refused. The seed is printed when an assertion fails.

## Non-Goals

- **Editing takes in place on take lanes.** After the PR #984 review F3 fix (story 328), non-COMP tools do not edit through take lanes; the Pointer only locates the playhead there. In-place lane editing is a separate future feature.
- **A range-overwrite swipe** ("copy selection to main"), which deliberately writes comp output over existing main-lane material.
- **Uncommitting by any route other than undo.** For example, a "resume comping and discard my edits" command. If it is wanted, it is a follow-up.
- **Automatic boundary crossfades between adjacent slices.** A crossfade the engine adds at every comp boundary by itself remains story 328's known limitation. Only a fade the engineer sets at a shared boundary becomes a crossfade (see Goals).
- **Lane persistence.** This is story 334. That story's take schema must persist the comp-owner marker on clip elements and this story's committed state. Otherwise the first swipe after a reload doubles the comp output, because the reloaded clips are untagged and the comp is live.

## Technical Notes

- Depends on story 328 (the unified take model, `TakeComping`/`CompRegion`/`CompileCompAction`/`SetCompRegionAction`, and the F4 comp-owner tag on `AudioClip`) and story 334 (persistence of the tag and the committed state).
- Code locators: `TakeComping` (regions, compile, its passes and `isSupersededOutput`, and the new committed state), `CompileCompAction` (live-track occupancy by owner tag), `Track.addClip` (the superseded-output fence), `AudioClip.trimmedCopy` (remnants that keep the tag), `ClipEditOperations.pasteOver`/`crop` and `TrackStripController`'s Paste Over, Crop and Split at Playhead items (daw-app), `SetCompRegionAction` and `CompToolHandler` (swipes, and the refusal path), `ClipInteractionController` (`handleCompPress`, `handleEraserPress`, the trim/move/slip gestures), `SplitClipAction`, `RemoveClipAction`, `ClipEdgeTrimAction`/`TrimClipAction`, `MoveClipAction`/`GroupMoveClipsAction`, `SlipClipAction`, `GlueClipsAction`, `SetClipGainEnvelopeAction`/`NormalizeClipGainAction`/`FadeClipAction`, `CrossTrackMoveAction`, `DuplicateClipsAction`/`PasteClipsAction`, `RenderInPlaceAction`, and `CompoundUndoableAction`/`UndoManager`.
- Prefer one seam over per-action special cases. A single classifier should map an undoable clip edit on tagged comp output to *neutral*, *translate* or *commit*, and wrap the edit and its comp change in one `CompoundUndoableAction` where the edit enters the undo manager. Sweep every action that removes, replaces or re-times a main-lane audio clip, including subtypes and gesture paths that build actions indirectly, so a new edit action cannot silently bypass the rule.
- Keep the story-328 discipline: compile from the live comping state, and never restore the main lane from a remembered clip list.
- **Open question: translate a cross-track move instead of committing.** The Goals table commits the comp on a cross-track move. A design option is to translate it, like an erase on the source and a copy on the target: on the source track, every `CompRegion` over the moved clip's range is removed or clipped, as an erase does, so the rest of the comp stays live there; on the target track, the clip is a user clip (tag cleared). The comp would be committed only when the move cannot be expressed that way. That fits a take-folder workflow, where an engineer moves one phrase of a lead comp to a doubles or effects-throw track and goes on comping the lead. Decide before implementation; if adopted, the cross-track move leaves the commit rows of Goals, criterion 4 and the "Every non-expressible edit commits" test, and gets a translated scenario of its own.
- **Open question: should a partial swipe clip the region it overlaps?** Today a swipe deselects every region it overlaps, whole: `TakeComping.addCompRegion` removes each existing region that overlaps the new one before adding it. So a swipe over the middle of a region silences the rest of that region's phrase, and `TakeCompingCompileTest.aSwipeAfterAScissorsSplitOfTheCompReplacesBothHalves` pins that behaviour (after the lane-2 swipe over beats 3-5, only beats 3-5 play). The "Split then swipe" test above, and the "Partial overlap" case of the gain test, assume instead that the swipe clips the overlapped region and keeps its remainders. Decide before implementation; if swipes keep deselecting whole regions, those two tests change to match.
- **Open question: dropping a file on a take lane.** Dropping an audio file on a take lane still lands it on the main lane, over the comp: `AudioImportController`'s drop resolves the track with `trackIndexAtY`, which includes the take lanes, while the Pencil is inert there. Decide whether a drop targets the track (as now, and then the drop is a user clip under the paste rule) or is refused or redirected over a take lane.
- Cross-refs: `docs/design/RECORDING_RELIABILITY_DESIGN_BOOK.md` §3.5 (the unified take model); story 328's Resolution, "Comping"; story 334 (`PERSISTENCE_INTEGRITY_DESIGN_BOOK.md`).
